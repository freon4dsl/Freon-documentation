# 1. Clarify Your Unique Value

What exactly does your workbench do that no other one does?

Can you phrase that in one or two short, compelling sentences?
That message will become the backbone of your promotion.

# 2. Start Small, with the Right Audience

Niche developers & researchers: Focus on people who already build DSLs, compilers, or modeling tools. They’re your early adopters.

Communities: Share in focused places like DSL-related mailing lists, forums, LinkedIn groups, Reddit (r/ProgrammingLanguages, r/Compilers), or Discords dedicated to programming language theory.

# 3. Create “Entry Points”

People will only try your tool if they immediately see what they can achieve with it. Consider:

A short tutorial (“Build your first DSL in 15 minutes”).

A live demo (online editor, video walkthrough).

Use cases that show how it solves real problems more easily than alternatives.

# 4. Leverage What You Already Have

Since you’re short on time and resources:

Documentation doubles as promotion: Well-written examples are shareable content.

Blog posts or short LinkedIn articles: Talk about what you learned while building it.

GitHub: Make your repo welcoming—clear README, starter examples, maybe a "quickstart" button.

# 5. Build Credibility with Low-Effort Content

Give a talk at a meetup or small conference (record it once, share it everywhere).

Comparisons: Write “Why we built this instead of X” (without bashing, just clarifying).

Highlight the unique features—people love novelty if it solves a pain point.

# 6. Grow Slowly but Steadily

You don’t need hundreds of users at once. A handful of engaged users who give feedback and spread the word are more valuable than a big but silent audience.
